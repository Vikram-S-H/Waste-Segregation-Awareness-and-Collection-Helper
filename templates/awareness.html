Why is Waste Segregation Important?

Waste segregation means separating different types of waste before disposal. It helps improve recycling, reduce pollution and protect our environment.

Four Categories of Waste

🌱 Biodegradable Waste

Waste that decomposes naturally.

Examples: Fruit peels, vegetable waste, leftover food and leaves.

Disposal: Compost suitable organic waste.

♻️ Recyclable Waste

Materials that can be processed into new products.

Examples: Paper, cardboard, glass bottles and accepted plastic containers.

Disposal: Keep materials clean and dry for recycling.

🗑️ Non-Recyclable Waste

Waste that cannot be recycled through available local facilities.

Examples: Certain contaminated packaging and mixed-material waste.

Disposal: Follow local municipal disposal instructions.

⚠️ Hazardous Waste

Waste that may contain harmful substances.

Examples: Batteries, paint, chemicals and certain electronic waste.

Disposal: Use authorised collection facilities.

Waste Segregation Tips

  • Separate wet and dry waste at the source.
  • Keep recyclable materials clean and dry.
  • Never mix hazardous waste with regular waste.
  • Reuse products whenever possible.
  • Compost suitable kitchen and garden waste.
  • Follow local municipal collection guidelines.

Remember the 3Rs

Reduce unnecessary consumption. Reuse products whenever possible. Recycle suitable materials responsibly.
